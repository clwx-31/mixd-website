Marketing Plan

How MIXD reaches its customers

Our strategy in five parts: what we want to achieve, who we sell to, the marketing mix, an honest look at our position, and what it costs.

01 — Objectives

What we are aiming for

Each objective is SMART: specific, measurable, achievable, relevant and time-bound.

🎯

Grow the drop list

Reach 25,000 email subscribers by the end of 2027, up from 9,400 today.

📈

Sell through faster

Sell 90% of every limited drop within 14 days of release, up from 72%.

🤝

Sign new partners

Secure eight new brand collaborations per year, with at least two outside apparel.

🔁

Keep customers

Raise repeat purchase rate to 35% within 18 months through the drop list.

🌍

Expand regionally

Ship to three new European markets by Q4 2027 using regional production partners.

♻️

Stay sustainable

Keep 100% of blanks certified organic or recycled while holding margin above 55%.

02 — Target market

Who we sell to

We segment the market four ways, then target one primary and one secondary group.

Segmentation type How we segment Our target
Demographic Age, income, occupation 16–28, students and early-career, disposable income $80+/month on clothing
Geographic Country and city type Urban Canada, US and Western Europe with reliable next-week delivery
Psychographic Values and lifestyle Value originality over logos; care about where clothing is made
Behavioural Buying habits Follow brands on social media; buy limited releases; shop mostly on mobile
Primary target

The collector

18–24, follows streetwear accounts, already owns collaboration pieces and checks release calendars. Buys two to four times a year, spends $60–$90 per order, and posts what they buy. This group drives our sell-through rate.

Secondary target

The gifter

25–40, buying for someone in the primary group. Less price sensitive, shops around November and December, and needs clear sizing and fast shipping more than it needs drop timing.

Positioning statement

For 16–28 year olds who want clothing nobody else has, MIXD is the apparel label that turns unexpected brand pairings into limited runs — because two brands you already know, mixed once and never repeated, beats one more plain logo tee.

03 — Marketing mix

The 7Ps

How every part of the business supports the same positioning.

👕

Product

Limited-run apparel from brand collaborations, plus an always-available core range. Every drop is numbered and never reprinted, which is what makes it worth buying now.

💷

Price

Premium pricing on drops ($32–$74) and cost-plus on the core range. We do not discount collaboration pieces — discounting would damage the scarcity the whole model rests on.

📍

Place

Direct-to-consumer through this website, plus three stockist pop-ups a year. Selling direct keeps margin and gives us the customer data the drop list needs.

📣

Promotion

Social media, the email drop list, and co-promotion with each partner brand. Partners post to their own audience, so each drop reaches people we do not pay for.

🧑‍🤝‍🧑

People

A six-person team plus freelance designers. Customer messages are answered within one working day by a real person, not an automated reply.

⚙️

Process

Pitch, licence, design, sample, pre-order, produce, ship. Pre-orders mean we only produce what has sold, which keeps waste and unsold stock low.

📦

Physical evidence

Recycled mailers, a numbered card in every order, and a woven label on every piece. The unboxing is part of what customers photograph and share.

04 — SWOT

An honest look at our position

Internal strengths and weaknesses, external opportunities and threats.

Strengths

  • Collaboration model gives us built-in access to partner audiences
  • Scarcity drives fast sell-through and full-price sales
  • Pre-order process keeps unsold stock and waste very low
  • Direct-to-consumer sales protect our margin

Weaknesses

  • Revenue depends on securing new partners every quarter
  • Small team means limited capacity during a drop week
  • No physical store, so customers cannot try sizes on
  • Organic and recycled blanks cost more than standard ones

Opportunities

  • Collaborations outside apparel — food, music, transit brands
  • European expansion using regional production partners
  • Growing demand for traceable, lower-waste clothing
  • Pop-up events as a low-cost route to new customers

Threats

  • Large brands copying the collaboration format at lower prices
  • Rising cotton and freight costs squeezing margin
  • Social platform algorithm changes cutting our free reach
  • A partner brand's reputation problem becoming ours
05 — Promotion

How one drop is launched

The same four-week cycle runs before every release.

1

Tease

Week 1. A single unbranded image posted by MIXD and the partner on the same day.

2

Reveal

Week 2. Partner named, product shots released, drop list gets the date first.

3

Pre-order

Week 3. A 72-hour pre-order window. Quantity produced is set by what sells.

4

Ship & share

Week 4. Orders ship, customer photos are reposted, and the drop closes for good.
