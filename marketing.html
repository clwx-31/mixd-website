Marketing plan

The plan behind MIXD

A focused strategy for customer choice, local awareness and responsible growth.

01 — Objectives

What we are aiming for

Each objective is SMART: specific, measurable, achievable, relevant and time-bound.

🎯

Build local awareness

Reach 500 Waukesha-area followers by the end of the first six months.

📈

Validate the product range

Collect at least 100 customer responses on preferred products, fits and colors in year one.

🤝

Test brand compatibility

Develop three sample outfits showing how MIXD pieces work with different brands.

🔁

Earn repeat customers

Reach a 25% repeat-purchase rate within 18 months through quality and service.

🌍

Build community impact

Contact Hope Center and complete one approved clothing drive during the first year.

♻️

Track product quality

Review every warranty claim and use the findings in a quarterly product improvement report.

02 — Target market

Who we sell to

We segment the market four ways, then target one primary and one secondary group.

Segmentation type How we segment Our target
Demographic Age, income, occupation 15–28, students and young adults who care about clothing and personal style
Geographic Country and city type Waukesha and southeast Wisconsin first, with online expansion across the United States
Psychographic Values and lifestyle Enjoy styling, thrifting and expressing individuality through what they wear
Behavioural Buying habits Shop on mobile, compare brands and want more control over fit and matching
Primary target

The style builder

A 16–24-year-old student or young adult who enjoys styling and thrifting, shops mainly on a phone and wants flexible pieces that work with clothing already in their wardrobe.

Secondary target

The practical buyer

A 25–40-year-old customer or gift buyer who prioritizes durability, clear sizing, dependable service and the confidence of a six-month warranty.

Positioning statement

For style-conscious young people who want more control over their wardrobe, MIXD provides durable, versatile essentials that mix easily across brands, supported by customer feedback and a six-month warranty.

03 — Marketing mix

The 7Ps

How every part of the business supports the same positioning.

👕

Product

Nine versatile categories: hoodies, shirts, pants, shorts, underwear, socks, beanies, crewnecks and tank tops, all designed to work across brands.

💷

Price

Value-based pricing reflects durable construction, useful choices and six months of warranty support while remaining realistic for students and young adults.

📍

Place

Direct-to-consumer through this website, beginning in Waukesha and southeast Wisconsin. Online selling keeps the experience simple and makes customer feedback easier to collect.

📣

Promotion

Short-form social content, school and community awareness, customer outfit posts and a proposed Hope Center clothing drive create relevant local visibility.

🧑‍🤝‍🧑

People

Jack, Massimo, Dillon and Ben lead the business together. Customer feedback is reviewed directly by the founders and used to improve the product range.

⚙️

Process

Browse, compare, choose, order and review. Clear product details reduce confusion, while post-purchase feedback supports continuous improvement.

📦

Physical evidence

Consistent black-and-purple packaging, clear care instructions, visible quality details and written six-month warranty terms reinforce the customer promise.

04 — SWOT

An honest look at our position

Internal strengths and weaknesses, external opportunities and threats.

Strengths

  • Nine-category range gives customers useful choice without overwhelming them
  • Versatile products can work across brands and existing wardrobes
  • Six-month warranty supports the quality promise
  • Founder-led feedback creates a direct customer connection

Weaknesses

  • New company with no established customer base
  • Four-person team has limited time and resources
  • No physical store, so customers cannot try sizes on
  • Nine categories create inventory and sizing complexity

Opportunities

  • Local awareness through schools and Waukesha community events
  • Customer feedback can reveal high-demand fits and categories
  • A Hope Center clothing drive can connect the brand to local impact
  • Online sales create room to expand beyond southeast Wisconsin

Threats

  • Established clothing companies compete on price and recognition
  • Material and shipping costs can reduce margins
  • Fast-changing trends can make inventory harder to predict
  • Product failures could create warranty costs and hurt trust
05 — Promotion

How MIXD enters the market

A simple cycle builds awareness, demonstrates choice and turns feedback into improvement.

1

Introduce

Week 1. Present the name, slogan, founders and customer problem through the website and social media.

2

Demonstrate

Week 2. Show sample outfits that mix MIXD pieces with different brands and personal styles.

3

Listen

Week 3. Survey potential customers about categories, fit, color, price and warranty expectations.

4

Improve

Week 4. Use results to refine the first products and explain what changed because of customer input.
